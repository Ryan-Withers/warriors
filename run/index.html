Special

  • The TE takes the linebacker lined up over him (the 90). In Nub he helps on the 5.
  • Luke's "Dallas Special vs Over" sketch is Duo to the TE: PST takes the 5T, PSG has the 3T solo, C and BSG double the 1T up to the Mike. The backside tackle is uncovered: step B gap first, then look for work. The backside end is the QB's man on the boot.
  • Away from the TE I applied the same idea: the Y takes the backside 90, the end next to him is the QB's, and the tackle beside him steps B gap and climbs.
  • Outside zone only changes the Y (straight to the 90), so the Z's -1 moves to the SS.
  • Special is a TE adjustment, so it's off with no TE.

Calls I made

Lines tagged inferred are where the playbook doesn't say. per Luke comes from the WhatsApp thread.

  1. Duo vs 5225: a head-up 2T counts as being in your BS gap, so he's yours and the C combos the playside 2T.
  2. Counter toward the TE (Bears from ACE RT): the Y's wrap rule is for when he's backside. Playside he blocks gap-down-backer like a tackle, and X and Z swap their counter jobs.
  3. Outside zone with no TE to aim at: the RB aims at the playside tackle's outside hip.
  4. No TE: the slot Y blocks like the H and takes the 90 over him. On counter that's the playbook's "unattached = leverage".
  5. Counter with one inside backer: the wrapper owns the Mike unless a free gap-down-backer lineman gets there first.
  6. Zone combos say "first one free climbs" because the rule is take over, knock over or climb.

Worth asking Luke

  • Duo into a 3T and 5T with no TE beside them (Dallas vs Under from ACE RT): the playside tackle's BS gap holds the 3T, so by the rule nobody has the 5T outside him. Only the QB's keep fake holds him.
  • Duo toward the TE without Special (Cowboys from ACE RT): the Sam at 90 is free against all three fronts. That's the hole Special fills by sending the Y to him.
